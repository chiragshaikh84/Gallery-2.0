01 / RECONSTRUCTING FORM

Built from countless white LEGO bricks, the sculpture transforms a modular material into the massive presence of a polar bear.

Each individual piece contributes to the larger silhouette, creating the illusion of fur, weight, and organic volume through repetition.

The restoration process preserves the clarity of the form while allowing the construction beneath it to remain visible.

POLAR BEAR — LEGO BRICK SCULPTURE
02 / CAPTURING MOVEMENT

The rigid geometry of LEGO bricks becomes the body, fur, and posture of a feline.

Thousands of repeated components work together to suggest muscles, curves, and movement, transforming a construction system into an expressive animal form.

Through careful restoration and surface reconditioning, the details of the sculpture become sharper and its sense of movement returns.

FELINE — LEGO BRICK SCULPTURE
03 / PATTERN IN REPETITION

Thousands of brightly colored bricks come together to recreate the intricate pattern of a peacock's feathers.

Individual pieces become pixels within a larger composition, turning repetition into a visual language of color, rhythm, and pattern.

What begins as a collection of simple manufactured components becomes an intensely detailed surface inspired by nature.

PEACOCK — LEGO BRICK SCULPTURE
04 / TEXTURE & CAMOUFLAGE

The spotted surface of the sculpture demonstrates how color, repetition, and placement can recreate the visual language of an animal's skin.

Each brick becomes a small unit within a much larger pattern, shifting the perception of the material from plastic construction to organic texture.

The result is a surface that only reveals its modular construction when viewed up close.

LEOPARD — LEGO BRICK SCULPTURE
05 / GEOMETRY TO NATURE

The rose reimagines a delicate natural form through a material associated with construction, repetition, and play.

Layered red LEGO bricks form petals that gradually unfold from the center, using simple modular geometry to suggest softness and movement.

The sculpture demonstrates how a limited set of repeated forms can produce something unexpectedly organic, complex, and alive.
